Social Media Data Collection and Privacy

Source 1

URLFTC Staff Report on Social Media and Video Streaming Surveillance
Site AuthorFederal Trade Commission
AbstractThis Federal Trade Commission report explains what the agency found after examining major social-media and video-streaming companies. It says that companies collected large amounts of information about both users and nonusers, including data from outside sources. The report connects these practices to targeted advertising, automated systems, and risks to privacy. It also discusses the limited control that people may have over the way their information is used or deleted. The source is useful because it gives an official government perspective on why social-media data collection matters. It supports the idea that clearer privacy rules, shorter data-retention periods, and better controls for users could reduce harm, especially for children and teenagers.

Source 2

URLNIST Privacy Framework
Site AuthorNational Institute of Standards and Technology
AbstractThe National Institute of Standards and Technology Privacy Framework is a guide for organizations that need to manage privacy risks while using data. Rather than focusing on one social-media platform, it explains a broader method for deciding what information should be collected, how it should be protected, and how people can be informed about its use. The framework emphasizes communication, accountability, and choices for individuals. This source is helpful for the privacy portion of the project because it shows that responsible data use involves more than a long privacy policy. Organizations need practical safeguards and a way to evaluate the effects of their data practices. These ideas support the paper's argument for transparency and meaningful user control.
