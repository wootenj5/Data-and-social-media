Social Media Algorithms and Targeted Advertising

Source 1

URLDigital Services Act Questions and Answers
Site AuthorEuropean Commission
AbstractThis European Commission page explains the Digital Services Act, a set of rules for online services that includes social-media platforms. It describes requirements for advertising transparency, protections for users, and limits on certain targeted advertisements. In particular, the page says platforms may not target advertisements using sensitive personal data, and profiling-based advertising is prohibited when a platform knows the person is a minor. It also explains that very large platforms must keep a public repository of advertisements and provide information about recommender systems. This source is useful because it connects algorithmic recommendations and advertising practices to public rules and accountability. It shows one way governments can require platforms to give people more information and protection.

Source 2

URLYouTube Performance FAQ and Troubleshooting
Site AuthorYouTube Help
AbstractThis YouTube Help page describes how YouTube uses recommendations to help viewers find videos. It explains that recommendations can depend on signals such as viewing history, searches, subscriptions, likes, dislikes, and feedback from the viewer. The page also shows that people can influence recommendations by managing their watch history and giving feedback. This source is useful because it provides a direct example of personalization on a major social-media and video platform. It shows why recommendations can be convenient: they can help users find material related to their interests. At the same time, it supports the paper's point that a personalized feed is not neutral, because the platform decides which signals are used to rank and present content.
